# An open letter to a lab that can answer the next question

To any cardiac ion-channel, protein-trafficking, electrophysiology, or iPSC laboratory with the capacity to test SCN5A R104Q:

My name is Ethan Bradley. I live with Brugada syndrome and carry the heterozygous SCN5A c.311G>A, p.Arg104Gln (R104Q) variant. The same variant is silent in my father, making this both a molecular question and a small natural experiment in incomplete penetrance.

This patient-led project asked why R104Q produces loss of function and whether that mechanism could be made experimentally testable. The resulting computational package nominates:

- an orphaned buried D84 charge as the structural lesion;
- D84N as a genetic suppressor hypothesis;
- ZINC000004286767 as one confirmed, deeply separated cation-clip gripper at n=3; and
- ZINC000016526277 as one conditional second candidate at n=2.

These are computational nominations. They are not medicines, measured affinities, evidence of rescue, or a reason to alter clinical care. My ICD and established care remain the protection. A clean negative experiment would be valuable and would close the present hypothesis honestly.

## The first ask: Experiment Zero

Before testing any compound, compare:

1. wild-type NaV1.5;
2. R104Q alone; and
3. WT + R104Q at 1:1 expression.

Measure surface-to-total NaV1.5 and peak sodium-current density. This resolves whether the heterozygous state is consistent with haploinsufficiency or whether R104Q exerts a dominant-negative effect. That answer determines whether a chaperone-rescue experiment is sensible or could backfire.

## Only if that gate clears: the controlled rescue panel

The next panel is already pre-registered:

- confirmed nominee ZINC000004286767;
- conditional nominee ZINC000016526277;
- an equimolar agmatine comparison for generic cationic or osmolyte behavior;
- the R104Q/D84N anchor mutant to test whether any effect depends on D84; and
- a matched scaffold-negative analogue to test whether the computational geometry gate predicts biology.

Any surface-expression signal must be confirmed by peak sodium-current density. Rescue that persists in D84N would falsify the proposed D84-grip mechanism even if the empirical effect remained interesting. Equal behavior of the scaffold-negative would falsify the geometry gate. Failure of the nominees across a credible exposure range would falsify the present rescue nomination.
